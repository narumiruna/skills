# Slide Color Strategies

Use these as starting points, then adapt to brand, content, accessibility, and the actual delivery environment.

## Dark Technical

A dark neutral background with restrained light text and a few muted accents.

**Fits:** code, terminals, architecture diagrams, controlled-light venues, and screen recordings.

**Risks:** washout in bright rooms, high print cost, weak separation among many data series, and low-contrast muted text.

Typical role direction:

- dark gray Background and slightly lighter Surface
- light Text Primary and quieter but still readable Text Secondary
- blue/cyan Primary/Secondary with a limited warm Accent

Measure each text/background pair. Test the exported deck in the target room before claiming projector performance.

## Light Professional

An off-white or light neutral background with dark text and restrained accents.

**Fits:** formal presentations, text or data-heavy material, bright rooms, documentation, and handouts.

**Risks:** glare in dark rooms, flat hierarchy, weak light-gray text, and brand colors that fail as text.

Typical role direction:

- light Background with a distinct Surface
- near-black Text Primary and accessible Text Secondary
- one darker Primary, a supporting Secondary, and a limited Accent

Test representative print and venue output before claiming suitability.

## Accent-Driven

A neutral base with one strong emphasis color and mostly typographic or spatial hierarchy.

**Fits:** narrative talks, product stories, keynotes, and slides with one dominant message.

**Risks:** insufficient distinction for complex data, accent overuse, and meaning conveyed by color alone.

Reserve the Accent for a small, stable set of meanings. Add chart or semantic colors only when content requires them and provide noncolor cues.

## Selection

Choose from content and constraints, not audience stereotypes:

| Signal | Likely start |
| --- | --- |
| code and diagrams dominate | Dark Technical |
| text/data, print, or bright room dominates | Light Professional |
| selective narrative emphasis dominates | Accent-Driven |

Mix strategies only when a concrete requirement justifies it; keep one coherent semantic role system. Supplied brand colors override preset aesthetics but not readability—move an inaccessible brand value to decoration or adjust surrounding roles.
