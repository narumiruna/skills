# Issue 9 Application Guide

This guide summarizes how to apply selected ASD-STE100 requirements in original wording.
It is not a substitute for the standard's explanations, exceptions, or dictionary.

## Source and Access

- Authority: ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, Issue 9, dated 2025-01-15.
- Source: [official Issue 9 PDF](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf).
- Publisher: Aerospace, Security and Defence Industries Association of Europe (ASD).
- Use the printed part, section, rule, or dictionary entry for citations; PDF viewer page numbers are different.
- The standard is copyrighted and its introduction prohibits unauthorized redistribution.
  Link to the official copy rather than bundling the PDF or reproducing its dictionary.
- Read the dictionary introduction before interpreting its entries.
  Text extraction can lose column boundaries, capitalization, and help notes; inspect the original page when the extracted entry is ambiguous.

## Vocabulary and Terminology

Check these items against part 1, section 1, section 9, and part 2.

- A dictionary entry does not necessarily approve a word.
  Uppercase headwords identify approved entries; lowercase headwords identify unapproved entries with suggested alternatives.
  This typography does not require uppercase prose.
- For an approved word, verify the part of speech, approved meaning, permitted forms, and entry-specific help.
  A familiar English meaning can still be unapproved in STE (rules 1.2–1.4 and 9.2).
- Use American English spelling unless an official directive requires another spelling (rule 1.14).
- Keep one technical name for one item and check established terminology against company, industry, or subject-field sources (rules 1.8–1.11).
- A technical noun must fit a category under rule 1.5.
  Record its source and category when its acceptance is uncertain.
  Do not use a technical noun as a verb (rule 1.7).
- A technical verb must fit the context and a category under rule 1.12.
  These categories cover manufacturing, computer processes and applications, applicable subject fields, and law or regulations.
  Use approved dictionary wording instead when it accurately communicates the same instruction or information.
  Do not use a technical verb as a noun (rule 1.13).
- A glossary entry alone does not remove the category and context checks.
  An unapproved dictionary word can qualify as a technical term in one context but not another (rule 1.6).
- Reconstruct the sentence if a suggested alternative changes its meaning or part of speech (rule 9.1).
  Do not create unapproved phrasal verbs by combining approved words (rule 9.3).
  Check permitted technical verb expressions against rule 1.12 rather than banning every multiword verb expression.

## Sentence Construction

Check part 1, sections 2–4.

- Limit multi-word nouns to three words (rule 2.1), subject to the technical-name treatment in rule 2.2.
  These are groups of nouns and/or adjectives that modify a head noun, usually the last word, and function as one part of speech.
  Do not apply this limit to an entire noun phrase with articles or prepositional phrases.
  For example, “the pressure in the left tank” contains separate groups, “pressure” and “left tank,” not one six-word multi-word noun.
  Use prepositions or clauses to clarify the relationships in overlong groups.
  For a longer established technical name, give the full name first, then use a clear shorter form or meaningful hyphenated groups (rule 2.2).
  Do not shorten an official name in a way that identifies a different item.
- Use approved verb forms: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future, and past participles as adjectives (rules 3.1–3.3).
  Avoid perfect or progressive constructions and complex auxiliary constructions (rule 3.4).
  Do not reject the simple passive permitted by rule 3.6 merely because it uses an auxiliary and a past participle.
- Restrict an `-ing` form to a technical noun or a modifier within a technical noun, subject to the rule's explanation (rule 3.5).
- Use active voice for procedures.
  Descriptive writing permits passive voice only when the agent is unknown (rule 3.6).
- Express actions with verbs instead of abstract noun constructions (rule 3.7).
- Keep necessary articles and other words; do not shorten text with contractions or omissions (rules 4.2 and 4.5).
- Use vertical lists for complex content, and connect related statements explicitly (rules 4.3–4.4).

## Procedures

Check part 1, section 5.

- Limit a procedural sentence to 20 STE-counted words (rule 5.1).
- Give one instruction per sentence, except actions that must occur simultaneously (rule 5.2).
  Do not turn simultaneous actions into consecutive steps.
- Use imperative instructions (rule 5.3).
- Put a condition before the command when the reader must know the condition first, with a comma between them (rule 5.4).
- Put instructions in steps, not notes; notes give information (rule 5.5).

## Descriptions

Check part 1, section 6.

- Introduce information gradually and maintain a clear logical progression (rules 6.1–6.2).
- Limit a descriptive sentence to 25 STE-counted words (rule 6.3).
- Group related information into paragraphs with one topic and no more than six sentences (rules 6.4–6.6).

## Safety Instructions

Check part 1, section 7, together with the applicable safety specification.

- Limit every sentence in warnings, cautions, and other safety instructions to 20 STE-counted words, including hazard and consequence explanations (rule 5.1).
- Use the risk label supported by the actual risk analysis (rule 7.1).
  Injury or death requires a warning; equipment damage alone calls for a caution; combined risks require a warning.
- Start with the preventive command or the condition the reader must know first (rule 7.2).
- Explain the supported hazard or consequence (rule 7.3).
- Do not downgrade a warning, remove a precaution, or add an unsupported consequence as a language edit.
  Flag missing or conflicting risk information for technical review.
- STE does not require uppercase safety text or define the project's layout.

## Punctuation and Word Count

Check part 1, section 8 before reporting sentence lengths.

- Do not use semicolons (rule 8.1).
  Use hyphens for directly related words, not to conceal an overlong sentence (rule 8.2).
- For a vertical list, count the introduction before the colon separately from each list item (rule 8.4).
  Apply the procedural or descriptive limit to each part.
- Treat parenthesized text as one word in the outer sentence, but also count the text inside as a separate sentence (rule 8.5).
- Count each number, number with its unit, abbreviation, alphanumeric identifier, quoted text, title, heading, and placard or label text as one word under rule 8.6.
  The title and heading categories are not limited to fixed text.
  Count each proper noun of an individual, group, organization, or geopolitical entity as one word.
  Do not extend this proper-noun treatment to other categories merely because a name has multiple words.
  Do not count document numbering that identifies a paragraph or step.
- Count hyphenated groups as one word (rule 8.7).
- Keep fixed quoted text intact.
  Its special counting treatment does not prove that the quoted text itself meets STE requirements.

## Final Verification

Compare technical content before checking style.
Then check vocabulary, grammar, terminology consistency, sentence limits, paragraph limits, and safety wording independently.
Consult all applicable rules for a full review; this selected checklist does not cover every explanation or general recommendation in section 9.
Report uncertain technical terms and inaccessible dictionary checks instead of treating them as passed.
